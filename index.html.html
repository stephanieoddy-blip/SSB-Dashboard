Common themes
Key patterns from free-response comments, backed by quantitative data signals and direct quotes
Meeting timing 14 comments across 11 specialties
📊 Q7 enjoyment avg 9.08 despite widespread scheduling friction — engagement remains high even when attendance is difficult. Boards with timing complaints still score Q7 at 8–9+.

"The scheduling of the meetings for our group is challenging for active clinicians: 4pm on alternating Mondays and Tuesdays. In order to adequately participate I have to clear my schedule of patients or run in and out of the meeting."

— Obstetrics

"I wish the time of the meetings was more friendly for west coast, it is during school drop off and I am not able to attend most meetings!"

— Critical Care Medicine

"Rotating meeting times make it impossible for me to join the meetings. Need a standard meeting time so I can arrange my schedule with some predictability."

— Pediatric Urology
Earlier roadmap input 11 comments across 8 specialties
📊 Q2 (roadmap priorities) avg 7.74 — lowest-scoring product question. Q3/Q5 (feedback opportunities) are notably higher at 8.37/8.41, suggesting the gap is about when input is sought, not whether it's welcomed.

"Projects come to us too late, do not have a full understanding of the issues facing radiologists and technologists, and are not typically changed before being released."

— Diagnostic Radiology

"Having a forum to review early concepts, share ICU-specific use cases, and provide clinical input before features are finalized would help ensure these tools are safe and clinically relevant."

— Critical Care Medicine

"There remains a downstream design approach in which Epic builds first and the steering board offers recommendations afterward, when in reality end users should be involved much earlier in the design process."

— Pediatric Endocrinology
Org adoption gap 18 comments across 14 specialties
📊 Q6 avg 6.51 — lowest-scoring question by a wide margin, 1.57 points below the next-lowest (Q2 at 7.74). Consistent across all three years: 6.39 (2024) → 6.24 (2025) → 6.51 (2026). Respondents frequently note this reflects institutional constraints, not the board's work.

"I see so many opportunities for improvement presented and none of them are available in my institution so it feels like the theoretical 'possible' and daily reality are pretty far apart."

— Critical Care Medicine

"The variability in Epic version status across organizations often prevents the real-time use of tools we help develop."

— Pediatric Cardiology

"We are on year 3 of trying to implement the retrospective ICU mortality prediction model and never have been able to get the new rounding checklist implemented."

— Critical Care Medicine
Compensation for board service 5 comments across 5 specialties
📊 Raised by respondents with avg scores ranging 6.5–8.7 — not correlated with dissatisfaction, but with high engagement and perceived value. Board members generating meaningful product input are raising this as a sustainability concern.

"If you provided FTE to the Board members, we could regularly give you an hour weekly for developers. Epic will get what it pays for — right now, Epic is using physician volunteers — it's hamstringing Epic's ability to build faster and better."

— Oncology

"It is standard for advisory board participation to be compensated. I believe Epic should compensate board members for their significant time and effort. We are generating significant value."

— Ophthalmology

"Board service is highly valuable! It would be nice if Epic considered some kind of compensation or honorarium for the time and energy given."

— Gynecology
Epic leads praised 22+ positive mentions across specialties
📊 Q10 (leads support) avg 9.00 — tied for highest question score alongside Q7 (enjoyment). Named Epic leads received specific recognition in at least 12 specialties. Strongest positive predictor of overall satisfaction.

"Lee is amazing and runs the meetings fast and efficiently. He asks all kinds of insightful questions and really listens."

— Rheumatology (avg 10.0)

"John, Dave, and their Epic team create an atmosphere for success and collegiality with the board. They advocate for the board and ensure employees who present keep track of the impact of our feedback."

— Hospital Medicine

"I cannot overstate how wonderful it is to work with Cat and Jason."

— Ophthalmology
